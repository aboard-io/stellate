the whole system

A fugue is one idea and a list of the ways it comes back.

That is not a simplification — it is the entire document:

subject   a short line, in ladder degrees
entries   [{ at, voice, transform, shift }]

Everything below derives from those two. Six sections, six options, and by the bottom of the page you have seen all of them.

option 1 · the subject

Draw the idea.

Eight slots up the side are the chord's own notes — its four tones, twice, an octave apart. Writing the subject in degrees of the chord rather than in pitches is what lets it survive underneath a moving harmony, which is the one thing a fugue subject must do. Tap a column to move its note; tap the lit cell to remove it.

option 2 · the answer

The second voice enters a fifth up.

Tradition calls the first statement the dux and its reply the comes — the same line, transposed. On this ladder the fifth of the voicing is slot 2, so the answer is +2 degrees: the classical rule is one integer. Move it and you hear why tradition picked that one.

option 3 · the voices

How many lines are arguing.

Each voice enters in turn, alternating subject and answer. Two is a conversation — a two-part invention. Three is the usual.

Three is the ceiling here, and that is a fact about registers rather than a taste. The engine offers two independent melodic slots; the third voice exists because the answer's transposition separates it from the first. A fourth would have to share a register and clamp, and a clamped subject is not the subject — which is exactly the bug that made the first version of this page not actually make fugues.

option 4 · stretto

Don't wait for it to finish.

Stretto is the next voice entering before the last one is done. It is the device every fugue saves for the end, because it is the proof that the subject fits against itself — and nothing else in the piece raises the pressure the same way. At 1 each voice waits its turn; at ¼ they pile in.

option 5 · the transforms

Six ways it can come back. There are no others.

Turn the subject upside down and you get inversion; play it backwards and you get retrograde. Each undoes itself, and they commute — so together with doing nothing they make a group of exactly four. Add a change of speed, augmentation and diminution, whose product is also nothing, and the vocabulary is complete. Every fugue ever written uses some of these six. Tap one to add it as a later entry.

option 6 · the instrument

Which sound plays it.

The counterpoint is written; this only decides who performs it. A fugue is drumless by construction here, so the anchor lends its instruments, its key and its harmony, and nothing else.
