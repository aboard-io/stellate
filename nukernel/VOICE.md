# VOICE.md — REACHING THE KNOBS

Paul, 2026-08-25, three asks that are one problem:

> *"give me some UX for editing the human voice."*
> *"I want to cut breath, add babble, add syllables, and on and on"*
> *"Give me some basic instrument editors. I feel also we're just not using FM enough."*

The second sentence decides the design. It names **parameters** — breath,
babble, syllables — and then says "and on and on". So this is not a casting
surface with a cast of singers on it: it is **the parameters this voice
declares, as controls**, and a curated mouth is a convenience that SEEDS those
controls and never stands in place of them.

The third ask is the same feature one level up. Twenty-six Faust voices are
seatable from this page and **every one declares sliders the page has never
offered**; a record's sound is whatever the anchor typed into `tone` and `set`.
One system at two scales, then: an instrument editor is the general case, the
sung voice is its deepest case, and the only thing the voice has that nothing
else has is **a vowel per syllable**.

Measured 2026-08-25: every count and range below comes from `node` driving
`state-engine.js pitchedUnit()` against `engine/faust/dist/*-meta.json`; the
audibility verdicts are the inventory's offline sweeps. I did **not** drive the
page in a browser — §11 says what that leaves open.

## 1. WHERE IT LIVES

**In the voice's own tab, under Sound, as one table, directly after the
instrument menu and directly before the engineer.** `ui/eight.js:4235` already
draws `sound.instrument` there and `:4256` already appends `engineer(panel, CTX,
voice.name)` — "a cantor's send is one more fact about the cantor, next to its
register and its throat, not a room next door". A throat's own knobs are the
same kind of fact, one step earlier in the signal path: **what the voice IS**,
then **what is on it**, then **where it sits**. Down the column in that order.

**It is Sound and not Sheet music, vowels included.** The tempting reading is
that a vowel per note is Material. It is not, and the reason is structural
rather than taste: a `material.cells` entry is **voice-agnostic and
alphabet-free** (`kernel.js:8`; `AXES.md`'s "Material does NOT depend on
Alphabet") and one cell can be read by several voices at once —
`document.js:345` hands the same phrase to every line that names it. Two voices
reading one cell sing **different words**. A vowel is a property of the throat,
not of the notes, so it belongs where the throat is. `AXES.md`'s Sound row is
already that: *"voices, treatment, balance"*.

**No new grammar.** The block is a `<table>` inside `pane()`, exactly the shape
`ui/engineer.js:200-300` builds for a channel strip: `th` the parameter, `td`
the control and its readout, a third cell for the derived value. One
`heading(panel, "the instrument")` above it — `"the mouth"` when the seated
module is `tract_voice`, `voice_lead` or `voice_choir`, which is a fact the
extracted table carries and not a branch anybody types.

## 2. THE CONTROL LIST IS EXTRACTED, NEVER TYPED

`nukernel/knobs.js` — **GENERATED BY `nukernel/knobs-extract.js`, DO NOT EDIT**,
the same UMD-with-a-banner shape `gates.js` already has. It is the only list of
what an instrument editor may draw, and no view module carries a second one.

**The extraction is a MEASUREMENT, not a parse.** A parser was tried and it is
wrong: `state-engine.js:1378` delegates `modeld` and `tb303` to helper functions
(`modeldUnit`, `tb303Unit`), so scraping `mp("x")` out of the case body reports
`modeld 0/17` when the truth is 11. The extractor instead **probes the parent**:

    for each dsp in to-engine.js SYNTH
      base = SE.pitchedUnit(SYNTH[dsp].role || "melody", {model}, {bpm:120,seed:1})
      for each candidate key k
        a = pitchedUnit(role, {model, [k]: lo}); b = pitchedUnit(role, {model, [k]: hi})
        record every field of `params` (and vowels/vowelEvery/freqMin) where a !== b

Candidate keys are the union of the module's own `*-meta.json` labels and every
identifier the parent reads as `mp("k")` or `m.k`. Ranges come from the meta;
the recorded lo/hi are what came back **out** of the parent, so a clamp narrower
than the module's declaration is already in the number.

It finds three things no hand-written table would have got right. **Renames the
SYNTH table does not carry**: `nasal` → `velum`, `wobbleBars` → `wobbleHz`,
`damp` → `ring`, `res` → `resonance` on the 303. **One recipe word meaning
different things on different voices**: measured, `fenvAmount` moves
`params.envAmount` on `juno60` (the filter envelope, loudly audible) and
`params.fenvAmount` on `pad_saw`, `vp330`, `solina` and `supersaw`, where the
inventory measured **0.0 dB across the whole range** — so the label comes from
the PARAM and the document key from the RECIPE WORD, and they are not the same
string. **Non-param reach**: `vowelEvery` moves `unit.vowelEvery`, `voice` moves
`unit.freqMin`/`freqMax` as well as `params.voice`.

**The measured census — 222 controls across 26 voices**, excluding `freq`,
`gate`, `gain` and `level` (§3): `tract_voice` **22** · `hammond` 18 ·
`juno60` 17 · `oberheim` 17 · `synclead` 13 · `ppg` 12 · `casiocz` 12 ·
`voice_lead` 12 · `modeld` 11 · `supersaw` 10 · `voice_choir` 10 ·
`stk_guitar` 9 · `gtr_amp` 9 · `vp330` 8 · `solina` 7 · `tb303` 5 ·
`lead_fuzz` 5 · `stk_piano` 5 · `mallet` 5 · `pad_saw` 4 · `bass_wobble` 4 ·
`bell` 2 · `organ` 2 · `choir` 2 · `bass_reese` 1 · `dx7_alg5` **0**.

**THE ORDER IS THE PARENT'S OWN.** Rows are emitted in the order the parent's
case names them, which is signal order for free: on the tract that reads driver
→ articulators → nose and hiss → glottis → wobble → envelope, and on the synths
oscillator → filter → envelope. Nobody types an order, and the board's "down a
column reads one channel's path in signal order" (`ui/engineer.js:22`) holds
here too.

**THE INERT LIST is an exclusion table keyed by `(module, param)`** — never by
word, for the `fenvAmount` reason above — and every row of it is a MEASUREMENT
from the inventory, carried into `knobs-extract.js` as data with its number
beside it:

    fm2op/fenvAmount fenvAttack fenvDecay   vp330/fenvAmount fenvAttack fenvDecay
    solina/fenv*  casiocz/fenv*  supersaw/fenv*  pad_saw/fenv*
    vp330/breath   (0.4 dB, split 0.013 across 0..1)
    vp330/detune   (0.2 dB)

`vp330/breath` is the costly one to leave out — it is the PAD reading of "synth
voice" and Paul will look for a breath knob there — so it is excluded anyway and
the row prints **"the VP-330's own breath control does almost nothing — 0.4 dB
across its whole range, measured"** where a disabled row prints its reason. A
knob that does nothing is worse than no knob.

`gain` and `level` are excluded for a different reason: **two owners**. The
voice already has a `level` (`document.js:63`; `synthRecipe`,
`to-engine.js:880`, writes `m.level` from it before the `set` loop could
overwrite it) and the board's fader is the other half. A third level control on
one channel is what the desk round spent a day deleting.

## 3. ONE KNOB, EXACTLY

Per row of `knobs.js`:

    { key, param, label, kind, min, max, step, init, why }

* **`kind: "number"`** → `range(...)` (`ui/eight.js:1007`) — a slider with an
  `<output>` and an `aria-valuetext` already. Its `fmt` argument is where **the
  readout says the musical thing**, and it is data on the row: `rate` reads
  `"3.6 a second"`, `vowelEvery` `"2 beats a syllable"`, `attack` `"30 ms"`,
  `cutoff` `"7.0 kHz"`, a plain 0..1 dial its own number. No invented units.
* **`kind: "word"`** → a `<select>`, because a numeric slider on a word is a
  silent lie. Measured: `voice` takes `"tenor"` → `params.voice 4`, `"bass"` →
  `1`, and **`voice: 2` falls to the default 4**. Three keys are words: `voice`
  (five throats, and it moves the compass — tenor 123–494 Hz, soprano
  247–1047 Hz), `waveform`/`wave`, and the vowels (§7, its own widget).
* **`why`** prints when the row is drawn disabled, and only then (§5).

The label is the **param's** plain English; the document key written is the
**recipe word**. `nasal` writes `nasal` and the row says "the nose".

**The document key.** `voice.set[recipeWord] = value`. That is the field
`document.js:63` already carries, `to-engine.js:882` already renames, and the
parent's case already reads. **No engine change and no new document key for any
of the 222.**

## 4. ABSENT IS TODAY, AND A HAND-TURNED KNOB BEATS A PRESET

The law is not invented here. `audio/desk.js:212`:

> *A USER VALUE IS AN ABSOLUTE OVERRIDE, never a sum: a set band replaces the
> derived band, … and clearing back to absent returns the derived answer — the
> dim-vs-lit law.*

Applied whole:

1. **A key absent from `voice.set` is the derived answer** — the anchor's mouth,
   the patch row, the parent's default, whatever it is. Verified in node:
   `toGenre` on `songs.js` TERMS with `voices[0].set` present and with it deleted
   differ in that one object and nothing else, and two untouched calls are
   byte-identical but for `__v`, the counter `push()` bumps.
2. **A key present is an absolute override.** Never a sum, never a scale.
3. **Clearing returns the derived answer.** A continuous slider has no empty
   detent — `ui/engineer.js:637 knob()` solves that for a detented list by
   seating "as it stands" at the default's own place in the run, and the trick
   does not exist for a range. So **the third cell prints the derived value**
   ("the record's own, 0.06") when the key is absent and carries a `clear`
   button beside your number when it is set: one gesture back to absent, with
   the number you are overriding on screen the whole time.

**How a preset and a knob coexist.** `genres.js MOUTHS` is a table of derived
answers and stays exactly that. A curated-mouth menu, if one is ever added here,
writes **nothing** into `voice.set` — it writes the mouth's NAME, resolved
through `to-engine.js voiceForInstr`/`mouthForInstr` as today. Every knob you
have not touched then shows that mouth's number as derived and every knob you
have touched keeps beating it. No second rule, and no "reset to preset":
clearing a key IS returning to the preset. Gate 1 in §10 is the proof.

## 5. WHAT A VOICE THAT CANNOT BE EDITED SAYS

The precedent is the board drawing dead controls disabled with the reason
printed; the counter-precedent is `ui/engineer.js:262-270`, where a refusal was
**deleted** the moment it stopped being true — *"a control that works needs no
apology."* Both apply. Four cases, and the block draws in all four:

| when | what the block draws |
|---|---|
| **a sampled instrument** (`nativeOf` returns null — `ahh_choir`, every GM id) | the heading, no rows, one `<p class="nu-why">`: **"a recording has one breath in it — this instrument is a recording, so there is nothing here to turn. The fader, the EQ and the sends are on the engineer below."** |
| **a modelled voice the parent reads nothing off** (`dx7_alg5`, and `organ` / `choir` / `bell` / `bass_reese` are down to one or two) | the rows it does have, then: **"the parent reads only `cutoff` off a pipe organ. The rest of the module's sliders exist and nothing carries them yet."** Named per dsp from the extracted count, so the sentence cannot go stale. |
| **a key that is gated by another key** (`tongue`, `tongueD`, `tongueL`, `lips`, `fricX` under `artic`; `rate` and `seed` under `babble`) | drawn **disabled** with its reason, in the sheet's own voice: **"the seeded driver has the articulators — turn babble down to shape them by hand"**, and **"the tube is one uniform pipe until `artic` opens — there is nothing for the hiss to sit in front of"**. |
| **a key that exists and does nothing** (the inert list, §2) | not drawn as a control at all; drawn as the reason alone. |

The third row is measured, not guessed: at `babble` 0.4 the tract's `artic` is a
10 dB control and at `babble` 1 it is a 0.1 dB one. Drawing five live-looking
sliders the driver has already taken is exactly the lie this page forbids.

## 6. THE VOICE, AS THE DEEPEST CASE — PAUL'S THREE VERBS

**BREATH is three different knobs on three voices and he will hit all three.**
The ranges are the MODULE's and they differ — `to-engine.js:690-698` already
carries the paragraph explaining why — so the slider reads its extracted row and
never a constant.

| voice | key | range | what the ends sound like |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tract_voice` | `breath` | **0..1**, derived 0.06 | 0 clean fold, 1 = +0.41 on the HF split. Unmistakable air. |
| `tract_voice` | `voiced` | 0..1, derived 1 | the other half. **0 is digital silence, −180 dB.** See below. |
| `voice_lead` | `breath` | **0..0.6**, derived 0.05 | split 0.08 → 0.19. Gentle by design; a whisper is not reachable. |
| `voice_choir` | `breath` | **0..0.6**, derived 0.08 | split 0.10 → 0.17. |
| `vp330` | — | — | excluded, with its number printed (§2). |
| any sampled voice | — | — | the sentence in §5. |

`voiced` is a real whisper knob and a real footgun. Drawn — a tube that can stop
phonating is the one thing no formant bank here can do — with its floor at
**0.02, not 0**, and the reason printed: *"take it right down and the tube
whispers; at zero it stops making a sound at all."* Clamping a control's floor
away from a silence the module allows is a departure, written down here rather
than discovered later.

**PAUL'S LIVE COMPLAINT IS UPSTREAM OF ALL OF THIS.** *"the solo voice has
returned to intense breathiness"* is `FAM_EQ.vox = { mid: -1.5, hi: 1 }`
(`audio/desk.js:221`) — a family EQ on the strip, one carve for every vocal in
the catalogue, currently a net **+1 dB shelf at 7.2 kHz on all of them**, and a
breath slider will not undo it. **My read: the EQ comes to rest and `breath`
becomes the control**, because `breath` is the thing that is actually air and
`FAM_EQ.vox` is the thing that is actually every vocal at once. Two controls for
one sensation, one invisible, is the double §2 refuses. One line in a file this
design does not own, so: a recommendation with its number, not part of the build.

**BABBLE is one knob, one voice, and it gates four others — so it is drawn as a
MODE, not as a slider among equals.** Derived 0.7 in the parent
(`state-engine.js:1602`), 0.82 in `PATCH_MOUTH.synth_voice`, 0.4 as the chant
seats it. At 0 it is a held vowel; at 1 the seeded driver owns the articulators
outright. Two rows, in this order:

    how the mouth moves    ( ) by hand   (•) it babbles      <select>
    how much               [------|-----]  0.40              <range>, drawn only
                                                             when "it babbles"

The amount is still the real `babble` number and still writes `set.babble`; the
mode is which end of it you are at (`0` exactly, or the number). The five
articulator rows are enabled under "by hand" and disabled-with-reason under "it
babbles" (§5), which is the whole point of drawing it this way. Nothing else in
the tree babbles: a filter bank cannot shut.

**SYLLABLES is three controls, and they are three different questions.** These
are the three most legible things in the inventory:

| the question | key | range | readout | measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| how fast the mouth moves | `rate` | 0.5..12 | "3.6 a second" | dips/s 0.00 / 0.18 / 0.36 / 0.91 / 1.27 / 1.82 at rate 0.5/1/2/3.6/6/12 (at `babble` 0.4); 0.36 / 1.09 / 2.00 / 6.36 at `babble` 1 |
| how long it stays on one sound | `vowelEvery` | 0.25..8 | "2 beats a syllable" | the melisma axis; `MOUTHS.syll` uses 0.5 (skiffle, pop) to 8 (motet, dreamchoir) |
| what the sounds are | `vowels` | §7 | the word | — |

`rate` is derived from the TEMPO when absent — two a beat, `state-engine.js:1613`
— so its third cell reads **"the record's own, 3.07 a second at 92"** and moves
with the tempo slider. That is the derived-value rule doing real work: the
number you are overriding is a function of another axis, and you can see it.

`seed` is the fourth and it is a **sentence**, not a number: seed 1 says the same
thing forever, four seeds gave four different renders, and seed 1 re-rendered
bit-identical. Drawn as a plain integer with the readout "take 1", "take 2", and
the reason beside it: *"which sentence the driver says. The same number says the
same thing forever."*

## 7. THE VOWELS

**The alphabet is five letters, `aeiou`, spelled once at
`state-engine.js:1037`.** The walk is `state-engine.js:2697`:

    const step = Math.round(b / u.vowelEvery);
    sets.vowel = u.vowels[((step % len) + len) % len];

Three facts, all good for a UI: it **wraps** (no end condition to design), it is
indexed by **musical position** and not note count (so the same bar sings the
same word twice, and the live window and the offline press choose the same
syllable), and `vowelEvery` is the ruler.

**The widget is the page's own grammar for a sequence: the step grid's shape.**
The word is an ordered sequence with repeats, so it is not a `<select multiple>`
(that is a set) and this page has no free-text control. So: **a slider for HOW
MANY syllables the word is (1..8), and one `<select>` of five vowels per
syllable**, in a little table with one row per syllable — one row, one control,
which is exactly what the rotated step grid already is. It reads with the
stylesheet off, it reads in a screen reader, and it can only produce words the
alphabet names.

Beneath it, one sentence, derived and not editable: **"a-e-o, then round again —
one every 2 beats, so bar 1 sings a, bar 2 sings o."**

**Two traps a builder must know, both verified in node.**

* **The rows are swapped.** `voice_lead`/`voice_choir` read CSOUND formant
  tables indexed **a-e-i-o-u**; `tract_voice` reads `tract.lib`'s table indexed
  **i-e-a-o-u**, remapped by `TRACT_ROW = [2,1,0,3,4]` (`state-engine.js:1111`)
  and again on the live half at `to-engine.js:735`. Measured: `"aeo"` becomes
  `[0,1,3]` on the singer and `[2,1,3]` on the tract. **A UI that writes a
  vowel as a NUMBER sings the wrong vowel on one of the two families and
  nothing ever fails.** The `<select>`'s option values are the LETTERS. This is
  not a preference; it is the reason the control is a select of letters.
* **`vowel` as a static `set` key is dead.** Both cases write `vowel: word[0]`
  and `mapEvents` overwrites it every note. Verified: sweeping `set.vowel` 0→1
  on the mouth moves no param at all. **The only way to say a vowel is the
  `vowels` string.** Same for `push` — velocity always wins, and there is no
  free workaround; if Paul wants "press harder" that is a velocity edit in the
  motif grid, not a knob here.

**AND THE ONE HAND-TYPED `set` IN THE TREE HAS BOTH BUGS IN IT.** `songs.js:267`
seats the chant's cantor with twelve numbers and **two are dead**: `vowel: 1.4`
(a number, for a key overwritten every note) and `push: 0.42` (velocity wins).
The first document opened in the editor will show ten live rows and two dead
keys sitting in `set` invisibly, so **the block prints them** under the table:
*"this record also says `vowel: 1.4` and `push: 0.42`. Neither reaches the
engine — the vowel walk and the note's velocity write both every note."* Same
plain voice `songs.js` used for the chant, and it is how a lie gets found.

**Under the stave as well, and the page's own law says which is which.** *The
transport feed may only write inside an element carrying `data-live`, and no
`[data-live]` holds a control* settles it: **the score may PRINT the syllables
and may not EDIT them.** So do both — edit the word in the voice's tab; print,
in a `<p data-live="score">` under the two-measure system, *"cantor, bars 5–6:
a e o a"*, derived by the same `Math.round(beat0 / vowelEvery)` the engine uses
so the two cannot disagree. It is **not** an abcjs `w:` lyric line: `ui/abc.js`
has no lyric support and adding one is separate work, and pretending to align
letters under noteheads without the engraver's help would be a picture that
lies where a sentence would not.

## 8. AUDITIONING — AND IT IS ALREADY BUILT

**`audio/audition.js` cannot do this and should not be asked to.** It holds its
own `AudioContext`, fetches GM piano zones and plays `toNotes()` output — *"a
musician leaning over and playing the tune once"*. It has no Faust in it at all.
A throat is not auditionable through it.

**But a knob turned while the record plays is heard on the next bar, with no
rebuild, and that path already exists.** Traced in source:

    a control calls changed()            ui/eight.js:703
      -> push()                          rewrites GENRES[...] with a fresh __v
      -> commit("box")                   ui/eight.js:386
      -> live.js on("box", changed)      audio/live.js:740
      -> compile()                       audio/live.js:738 — only while playing
      -> barPlan(n).units                audio/plan.js:536
      -> "per-unit param GLIDE from the (possibly morphed) unit spec"
         stream-renderer.js:806-812 — changed keys only, setParamValue on the
         LIVE procs, and the module's own si.smooth ramps the step

So the answer to "can a knob be heard without playing the whole record" is
**press play, turn it, hear it at the top of the next bar**, at a cost of one
`compile()` per gesture — which every other control on this page already costs.

Two exceptions, stated on the page rather than left to be discovered. **Changing
the instrument is a new stream**, not a glide — the module set is the stream
signature (`stream-renderer.js:173`) — which is the instrument menu's existing
behaviour, unchanged. And **`voice` moves the compass**, so the line is
re-folded: it lands on the next bar like everything else, but the notes move as
well as the colour.

**So: no audition button in this block.** A second way to hear one voice is a
second engine on the page, and the one-engine law is why this box sounds the way
it does. "Play this voice alone" already exists on the board — `alone`,
`ui/engineer.js:291`.

## 9. FM — THE HONEST ANSWER, WITH THE BUDGET

Paul is right that FM is underused and wrong about why. Measured:

* `COST.fm2op` = **0.53**. `DX7_COST` = **6.4** (`state-engine.js:1890`).
  `BUDGET` = **40**. So an `fm2op` is **1.3% of the record's whole voice
  budget** — cheaper than `pad_saw` (1.0), cheaper than everything except
  `organ` and `choir` — and one DX7 voice is **16%**, against `tract_voice`'s
  24%.
* **`fm2op` is not seatable at all.** It has no `SYNTH` row
  (`to-engine.js:237-296`) and no `PATCH_*` row. There is no string a document
  can write that reaches it. That, and not cost, is why FM is shy.
* **The only FM this box can name is `dx7_alg5`, pinned to one preset out of a
  bank of 114, with `params: {}`** (`state-engine.js:1433`). Seated by exactly
  one anchor in 139: `vaporwave` (`genres.js:555`).
* **And it has been turned down.** The parent ships `idx1: 1.0` on an FM lead
  and `0.9` on a pad (`state-engine.js:1430-1432`), and at that index sweeping
  `ratio` across its whole 0.25..8 range moves the centroid **254 → 388 Hz** —
  nearly nothing. FM here is not broken; it is quiet.

**The proposal, in three steps, cheapest first.**

1. **One line, today: `SYNTH.fm2op = { model: "fm" }`.** That makes the
   0.53-cost FM voice nameable by any document, and it appears in the
   `sound.instrument` menu with no other edit — `instrOptions`
   (`avail.js:423-441`) reads `env.fleet`, which is `SYNTH_NAMES()`. Verified:
   the `fm` case builds `module: "fm2op"` for both a lead and a pad.
2. **Four `mp()` calls in the parent** at `state-engine.js:1430-1432`:
   `ratio` (0.25..8), `idx0` (0..8, the ATTACK index — flat at t=1 s and
   250 → 1012 Hz at t=30 ms, which is why a naive sweep calls it dead),
   `idx1` (0..8, the sustain index and the brightness knob, centroid
   236 → 1174 Hz) and `idxTime` (0.01..4, how fast one becomes the other). Raise
   `idx1` to 6 and the same `ratio` sweep gives 34/19/11/17/7/9/7/5/5 partials
   with 26/10/0/14/0/4/0/0/0 inharmonic — integer ratios are tones, 0.25 / 1.4 /
   3.5 are bells. Without these four there is no FM editor, only an FM
   on-switch: `fm2op` reaches **5** controls today and four are `attack`,
   `cutoff`, `vibRate`, `vibrato`. With them it reaches nine, they are the right
   nine — **three knobs and a time** — and they come out of §2's table with no
   special case.
3. **114 presets behind a lead chair, and this one needs NO engine change at
   all.** `state-engine.js:1229` fires **before** the model switch and takes
   `m.dx7 = { algorithm, params }`; `synthRecipe` (`to-engine.js:882`) copies
   `set` keys straight onto `m`; `engine/faust/data/dx7-presets.json` is exactly
   the `{alg, params}` shape, 114 entries, full nested addresses. **Verified in
   node:** `pitchedUnit("melody", { model: "rhodes", dx7: { algorithm: 12,
   params: {…} } })` returns `module: "dx7_alg12"` carrying those params. So a
   `<select>` of 114 cartridge names on a `dx7_alg*` voice is a `knobs.js` row
   of `kind: "word"` and nothing else.

   Why the DX7's own 147 operator sliders cannot be knobs, so nobody tries: the
   generic writer addresses params as `"/" + root + "/" + label`
   (`stream-renderer.js:83`) and the DX7's addresses collapse to 32 unique last
   segments — six operators all have an `L1`. Only `dx7Params`, with full paths,
   can reach them. **A preset menu is the editor for a DX7**, and 114 is not a
   small vocabulary.

**And the honest budget sentence, for the page:** *"a DX7 voice costs 6.4 of the

## 11. WHAT I DO NOT KNOW

* **Whether the tract sustains on the live path.** Held 18 seconds offline at
  the chant's own twelve numbers it is a 3 dB wobble, not a decay — so the
  chant's own comment (`songs.js:252`, *"it sounds for about six seconds and
  then decays to silence"*) does not reproduce in node. The module sustains; the
  record did not. It is `pool: 1, mono: true` (`state-engine.js:1587`) and the
  seam is the likeliest suspect. **Until somebody re-runs it in a browser the
  block prints the caveat where a builder would want a preset:** *"held tones on
  this throat have gone quiet on this page before. It is a talker, not a
  drone."* Implying you can hold a note on the tract would be a lie; calling the
  tube broken when the module is not would be another one.
* **Whether `vibrato`, `vibRate`, `vibRise`, `glide`, `spread` and `drift` do
  what they say.** They are pitch and time and stereo; the inventory's mono
  offline metrics cannot see them, and a zero-crossing wobble measure is
  unusable on a waveguide (476 cents of baseline jitter). They are **not
  inert** — the probe shows them reaching real params — so they are drawn, and
  the gate's assertion for them is reach, not audibility. Somebody with ears
  should sweep them once.
* **How 22 rows read on a 390px phone.** I did not measure it; the Hammond's
  nineteen drawbars in the same table shape are the closest thing to prior art.
