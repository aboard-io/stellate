⟳ why the lane stands upright

“Top to bottom is always better than left to right” — Paul, 2026-08-27. Time runs downward on every grid, roll and lane; only the engraved score keeps its horizontal law.

This half-returns to the tracker orientation we reversed on 2026-08-15. What does NOT return is the cell-per-value tracker text grid — the lane keeps direct manipulation: one pill per note, painted harmony, gestures instead of widget stacks.

1

Columns are the Alphabet. Seven degree columns (and the leading tone below) come from g.scale/mode, low on the left like a keyboard; a chromatic passing tone is not a hidden column, it is a different Alphabet. Snapping is the editor, not a validator.

2

Overlay, not a second copy. The composed version is dashed meter-green ghosts over the same lane — engine truth, byte-equal to what the score plays. The eye compares positions, not two staves. Captions carry the tense: “as played in chorus — you are writing psalm.”

3

Chord windows are painted, not narrated. chordsOf() already returns start/len/deg/quality per window; each window is a horizontal band of rows, tinting its chord-tone columns green and the leading-tone column flag-yellow under V. The header’s role marks change as the band changes.

4

Velocity is ink depth on the pill — deeper cobalt plus the printed v-number — never a second slider column. Accent is the yellow flag ring (a categorical event, not a level; kernel.js keeps them distinct and so does the eye). Duration is the pill’s height: a long note is a tall pill.

5

The playhead lives in its own [data-live] layer above the cells — a 4px clock-red line descending. The clock may move that line and the caption beside the play button — nothing else. Edits mutate pills in place; the lane is never rebuilt under a finger (MOTIF.md’s 114px jump becomes structurally impossible).

6

The tri-state button keeps yesterday’s law: the word on it is the NEXT tap (▶ play → ↻ loop → ■ stop); the [data-live] sentence beside it is the only text the sound’s own callback may write.

proposed · the kit

Drums are columns — kick, snare, hat, open, perc across — and steps fall down. Velocity is the width of each hit, the groove is rows that physically sit late, and the fill is the bottom of the phrase, hatched where it lands7.

7

Depth, not a second grid. Tap cycles rest → ghost → hit → accent; press-and-drag sideways writes any level 0–9 as width. The grid reads exactly like the engine does — “a ghost is a 2 and an accent is a 9” (drums-kit.js).

8

Swing is displacement, displayed. The “&” rows (label, cells, hits — the whole row) physically sit late; the grey dashed stub marks straight time where they left it. The rows move by transform only — the DOM is never rebuilt.

9

The fill is a place, not a mode. The phrase strip shows all four bars; bar 4 is hatched because a fill is a SHAPE over the phrase — the bottom quarter — and “fourth measure fill” lands there and nowhere else. Open bar 4 and the fill’s ground is hatched from its entry down, its strokes flag-yellow; bars 2–3 say “= 1” instead of repeating themselves.

10

Same bench, two kinds. Lane and kit share the toolbar, the tri-state play, the ghost-overlay idiom, the 44–48px step rows and the header row of names — a drum pattern is not a second app.

the laws it keeps

DeterminismEvery displayed fact is a table read: chord bands from chordsOf, roles from pc-set membership, overlays from the OPS words, ms from bpm. No model, no guess.
[data-live]The clock owns the falling red line and one sentence. It may not move a pill, a cell, a scroll position, or the button’s word.
Extraction, never by handThe composed overlay is the score’s own operator applied to the cell — if the overlay and the record ever disagree, the gate fails, not the eye.
Down is timeSteps fall; the needle falls; the fill is the bottom of the phrase. No surface here scrolls sideways — vertical space is cheap, width is not.
One owner per factVelocity lives in pill ink / hit width and nowhere else; duration lives in pill height; the tie and dot radios retire.
Chunk, zero hairlinesStep rows are 48px tall with full-width tap areas; every rule is 2px ink or more, 3px at beats and edges; chips, buttons and thumbs meet --ctl. Plain HTML: divs, CSS grid, one hand-drawn SVG — the gate can read every fact from the DOM.
Design artifact only — nothing here is wired to the engine. The Bench replaces the cell-row and step grid inside the Sheet-music axis of ui/eight.js; the staff engraving stays, above, as the composed view’s printed form — the one surface allowed to run left to right.
