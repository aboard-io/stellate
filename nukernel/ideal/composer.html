1

Rows are the Alphabet. Seven degree rows (and the octave fold) come from g.scale/mode; a chromatic passing tone is not a hidden row, it is a different Alphabet. Snapping is the editor, not a validator.

2

Overlay, not a second copy. The composed version is ghost pills over the same lane — the eye compares positions, not two staves. Captions carry the tense: “as played in chorus — you are writing psalm.”

3

Chord windows are painted, not narrated. chordsOf() already returns start/len/deg/quality per window; the lane tints chord-tone rows green inside each window, and the leading-tone row amber under V. The row labels’ role dots change as the window changes.

4

Velocity is depth on the pill — ink intensity plus the printed v-number — never a second slider column. Accent is the amber ring (a categorical event, not a level; kernel.js keeps them distinct and so does the eye).

5

The playhead lives in its own [data-live] layer above the cells. The clock may move that 2px line and the caption beside the play button — nothing else. Edits mutate pills in place; the lane is never rebuilt under a finger (MOTIF.md’s 114px jump becomes structurally impossible).

6

The tri-state button keeps yesterday’s law: the word on it is the NEXT tap (▶ play → ↻ loop → ■ stop); the cue-blue sentence beside it is the only thing the sound’s own callback may write.

Proposed · the kit

one grid — velocity is the fill-depth of each hit, the groove is drawn on the ruler, the fill is a bar you can see7

the hands

the KITOPS words — each one a deterministic table op over the grid; tapping one shows its result as the same cue-blue ghost layer the lane uses

7

Depth, not a second grid. Tap cycles rest → ghost → hit → accent; press-and-drag vertically writes any level 0–9. The grid reads exactly like the engine does — “a ghost is a 2 and an accent is a 9” (drums-kit.js).

8

Swing is displacement, displayed. The amber ticks (and the columns under them) physically sit late; the grey stub marks straight time. Slide the fader and watch the “&” columns lean. The bar’s cells move by transform only — the DOM is never rebuilt.

9

The fill is a place, not a mode. The phrase strip shows all four bars; bar 4 is hatched because a fill is a SHAPE over the phrase (“fourth measure fill” lands there and nowhere else). Open it and the fill strokes render amber; bars 2–3 say “= 1” instead of repeating themselves.

10

Same bench, two kinds. Lane and kit share the toolbar, the tri-state play, the ghost-overlay idiom, the 48px step and the sticky label column — a drum pattern is not a second app.

The laws it keeps

DeterminismEvery displayed fact is a table read: chord windows from chordsOf, roles from pc-set membership, overlays from the OPS words, ms from bpm. No model, no guess.
[data-live]The clock owns the playhead line and one caption. It may not move a pill, a cell, a scroll position, or the button’s word.
Extraction, never by handThe composed overlay is the score’s own operator applied to the cell — if the overlay and the record ever disagree, the gate fails, not the eye.
One owner per factVelocity lives in the pill/hit depth and nowhere else; duration lives in pill length; the tie and dot radios retire.
44px, zero exceptionsSteps are 48px wide; every pill, cell, chip and the range thumb meet --tap. Degree rows are 36px with full-height hit areas.
Plain HTML firstDivs and one hand-drawn SVG sparkline. No canvas framework; the grid is CSS grid; the gate can read every fact from the DOM.
Design artifact only — nothing here is wired to the engine. The Bench replaces the cell-row and step grid inside the Sheet-music axis of ui/eight.js; the staff engraving stays, above, as the composed view’s printed form.
