The Bench

Every step is one line. Play, hold, or rest; a pitch bar that grows from the center, −12 to +12, snapped to the scale while the lock is on; a weight bar, 0 to 7 — sixteen lines falling down the page in the order they will sound, chunky enough for a thumb. Change the scale and the lattice redraws; unlock it and accidentals land loud in flag yellow.

proposed · the motif editor

Sixteen steps, one line each. A row states its kind — play, hold, or rest — then its pitch as a bar growing from the marked zero, landing only on the scale's own detents while the lock is on1, then its weight, 0–7. The pitch badge's border is its role under the chord that will be sounding3; the red line is the clock, falling.

⟳ why the rows came back

“The original button structure with sliders was more novel and comprehensible.” — Paul, 2026-08-27. The pill lane retires. The shipped row-per-step idiom returns as the proposal — same bones, Enamel body: 48px controls, painted harmony, one knowledge rail, and time still runs down.

What does not return: the 12px radios, the 36px sliders, the five-widget clutter per row. One segmented kind button, one snapped stepper, one velocity control — and the controls of a silent row go faint instead of vanishing, so nothing ever reflows under a finger.

2026-08-27, later the same day: “Tighten it up. play/hold/rest, pitch offset −12 to 12, velocity 0 to 7, tightened to one line.” — the rows stay, each one squeezed to a single 52px line.

1

The bar IS the Alphabet. Locked, the landable detents are exactly the scale's pc set laid across two octaves — aeolian lands 0 2 3 5 7 8 10 12 and their negatives, pentatonic fewer, whole tone evenly spaced — and they are drawn as ink ticks on the bar, so the lattice is visible and changes when the scale does. Snapping is the editor, not a validator.

2

The lock is the honesty line. IN THE SCALE is today's kernel verbatim — deg is a scale degree and the Alphabet is the lattice (kernel.js, AXES: the Alphabet axis). ACCIDENTALS ALLOWED is not free: it needs the alphabet extension the review scoped — cents-valued alphabets, a chromatic channel beside deg — which is why the lock defaults ON: the locked mode ships first, the unlocked mode is a purchase.

3

Roles shrink to a border. chordsOf() gives the windows — i over steps 1–8, V over 9–16 — and the pitch badge's border is its role under the window it sounds in: green chord tone, yellow leading tone, plain color tone. Tap the row and the wisdom rail still speaks the whole sentence.

4

Hold inherits; geometry holds. A hold row draws the pitch and weight of the note still ringing, faint; a rest fades both bars behind one measured line saying why they sleep. Kind never changes the row's geometry — one 52px line, sixteen times — so the thumb's map of the bar holds.

5

The playhead lives in its own [data-live] layer — a 4px clock-red line descending over the rows. The clock may move that line and the sentence beside the play button; it may not press a button, move a bar, or scroll the page. The tri-state button keeps its law: the word on it is the NEXT tap (▶ play → ↻ loop → ■ stop).

6

The overlay survives as a marker. A small dashed meter-green tick under the step label of every row where the chosen development lands a note — the same reverse() the score runs, applied to this cell, byte-equal to what will play.

Tap cycles rest → ghost(1) → hit(4) → accent(7) → rest, and a sideways drag (pointer-captured) writes any of the eight. 9

The fill is a place, not a mode. Bar 4 in the phrase strip is hatched because a fill is a SHAPE over the phrase — the bottom quarter. Open it and the ground is hatched from the fill's entry down, its strokes flag-yellow; bars 2–3 say “= 1” instead of repeating themselves.

10

Same bench, two kinds. Motif and kit share the row-per-step spine, the toolbar, the tri-state play, the 44–48px targets, the faint-not-hidden law, and the one 0–7 velocity vocabulary. A drum pattern is not a second app.

resolved · the velocity question

The verdict. Paul's spec — “play/hold/rest, pitch offset −12 to 12, velocity 0 to 7, tightened to one line” — settles the A/B question this section used to argue: a nine-rung ladder cannot live on one 52px line, so the bar ships and the ladder retires. The bar's tap path is the whole musical vocabulary — ghost(1) → hit(4) → accent(7), then back where it started, never to silence — its drag writes the other levels, and 0–7 is the one range everywhere, motif and kit alike.

one gesture grammar

Thumb at 390px and mouse at 1280px perform the same sentence; nothing needs a hover to exist, nothing needs a drag to be usable. Every drag surface takes pointer capture and declares its touch-action, so the page scrolls from everywhere else and never from inside a control.

the laws it keeps

DeterminismEvery displayed fact is a table read: chord windows from chordsOf, roles from pc-set membership, detents from the scale's pc set, markers from the OPS words, ms from bpm. No model, no guess.
[data-live]The clock owns the falling red line and one sentence. It may not press a button, rewrite a row, or move the scroll.
Extraction, never by handThe development marker is the score's own operator applied to this cell — if marker and record ever disagree, the gate fails, not the eye.
Down is timeSteps are rows; rows fall; the needle falls; the fill is the bottom of the phrase. Vertical space is cheap, width is not — which is why a step is one line.
One owner per factPitch is the bar's fill from center and its badge, nowhere else. Weight is the bar's width and its printed number. Duration is which rows say hold. Kind is one segmented button.
The touch lawEvery drag surface: setPointerCapture on pointerdown, value from the pointer against the control's own rect, touch-action declared on the control and only the control. The native input stays as the keyboard's channel — pointer-events: none, one paint path for both.
Design artifact only — nothing here is wired to the engine. The Bench replaces the cell-row and step grid inside the Sheet-music axis of ui/eight.js; the staff engraving stays, above, as the composed view's printed form — the one surface allowed to run left to right.
