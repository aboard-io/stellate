proposed · the motif editor

Sixteen step rows fall down the page. Each row states its kind — note, rest, or tie — then its degree, snapped to the Alphabet so a wrong note is unenterable1, then its weight. The colored tick beside the degree is its role under the chord that will be sounding2; the red line is the clock, falling.

⟳ why the rows came back

“The original button structure with sliders was more novel and comprehensible.” — Paul, 2026-08-27. The pill lane retires. The shipped row-per-step idiom returns as the proposal — same bones, Enamel body: 48px controls, painted harmony, one knowledge rail, and time still runs down.

What does not return: the 12px radios, the 36px sliders, the five-widget clutter per row. One segmented kind button, one snapped stepper, one velocity control — and the controls of a silent row go faint instead of vanishing, so nothing ever reflows under a finger.

1

The stepper IS the Alphabet. − and + walk g.scale/mode degrees only — 7̂₋ below the tonic up to 7̂ — so snapping is the editor, not a validator. A chromatic passing tone is a different Alphabet, not a hidden value between the detents.

2

Roles are painted, not narrated. chordsOf() gives the windows — i over steps 1–8, V over 9–16 — and each row's tick is its degree's role under the window it sounds in: green chord tone, yellow leading tone, hollow color tone. Move the degree and the tick answers.

3

Silent rows stay put. A rest or tie row keeps its degree and weight controls, faint, with one measured line saying why they sleep — the row's geometry never changes when its kind does, so the thumb's map of the bar holds.

4

One knowledge rail. Tap any row and the wisdom line below the bar speaks: step, degree, role under the current chord, weight. One place, one sentence — not sixteen tooltips.

5

The playhead lives in its own [data-live] layer — a 4px clock-red line descending over the rows. The clock may move that line and the sentence beside the play button; it may not press a button, move a bar, or scroll the page. The tri-state button keeps its law: the word on it is the NEXT tap (▶ play → ↻ loop → ■ stop).

6

The overlay survives as a marker. The pill layer's ghost pills are gone; what remains is a small dashed meter-green tick under the step label of every row where the chosen development lands a note — the same reverse() the score runs, applied to this cell, byte-equal to what will play.

the velocity strategies

Two candidate shapes for the per-step level, both live on this page — try them with a thumb. Each is shown on a motif row and on a kit row. One is marked; the argument is under each.

A · the velocity bar RECOMMENDED

The bar's width IS the value and the number prints at its end — sixteen of them read as a bar-graph of loudness falling down the page, the same idiom the kit's cells already draw. Tap cycles ghost(2) → hit(5) → accent(9), so the no-drag path alone covers the three levels that matter; the sideways drag (pointer-captured, touch-action: none on the bar only — the fix that made today's sliders touchable) writes the other seven. If a drag ever fails on a phone again, this control degrades to exactly the shipped grammar. One gesture, zero extra chrome, one owner per fact.

on a motif row — drag sideways, or tap to cycle

on a kit row — same gesture inside each cell

B · the ladder

Every value a visible tap target, no drag at all — nine rungs, tap one and the rungs below it fill; tap the top filled rung again for silence. The honest answer to a month when drags didn't work. The cost is geometry: nine legal 44px rungs make one step over 100px tall and the bar two and a half phone screens; shown here at 9px rungs — real but thin — with slide-over allowed as a mercy. It is fully discrete and fully tappable, and slightly wider per row.

on a motif row — tap a rung

on a kit row — a ladder per cell

The call: A. The bar is one owner per fact (width = level = printed number), it survives a phone that refuses to drag because its tap path is the whole musical vocabulary, and down the page it reads as data — a dynamics curve you can see at arm's length. B's promise of “every value visible” breaks against the 44px law: honest rungs make the bar three screens tall; thin rungs betray the one law this rebuild exists to keep.

one gesture grammar

Thumb at 390px and mouse at 1280px perform the same sentence; nothing needs a hover to exist, nothing needs a drag to be usable. Every drag surface takes pointer capture and declares its touch-action, so the page scrolls from everywhere else and never from inside a control.

the laws it keeps

DeterminismEvery displayed fact is a table read: chord windows from chordsOf, roles from pc-set membership, markers from the OPS words, ms from bpm. No model, no guess.
[data-live]The clock owns the falling red line and one sentence. It may not press a button, rewrite a row, or move the scroll.
Extraction, never by handThe development marker is the score's own operator applied to this cell — if marker and record ever disagree, the gate fails, not the eye.
Down is timeSteps are rows; rows fall; the needle falls; the fill is the bottom of the phrase. Vertical space is cheap, width is not.
One owner per factVelocity is the bar's width and its printed number, nowhere else. Duration is which rows say tie. Kind is one segmented button, not three radios.
The touch lawEvery drag surface: setPointerCapture on pointerdown, value from the pointer against the control's own rect, touch-action declared on the control and only the control. The native input stays as the keyboard's channel — pointer-events: none, one paint path for both.
Design artifact only — nothing here is wired to the engine. The Bench replaces the cell-row and step grid inside the Sheet-music axis of ui/eight.js; the staff engraving stays, above, as the composed view's printed form — the one surface allowed to run left to right.
