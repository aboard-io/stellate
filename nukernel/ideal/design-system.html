the nukernel design language

Enamel

Bright flat paint on a steel chassis. Four loud colors, each with one job. Jet borders you could grab, shadows with no blur, type with real weight. Time runs downward everywhere but the engraved score. No knobs — every continuous control is a vertical slider. Lampblack is retired.

A bright chassis, white plates, jet ink — and four paints. A paint is semantic, never decorative, and paints never mix: a thing is colored because the hand set it, the clock is on it, the engine measured it, or it is armed and waiting.

the flag

Cobalt is the hand. Selection, focus, the chosen word, the filled part of a slider, a lit step in the grid. If cobalt is on it, you put it there.

Red is the clock. The playhead row, the sounding note, the [data-live] sentence. If red is on it, it is happening right now and it will move without you.

Green is the meter. What the engine is actually doing — measured, never trusted. A slider with no green beside it is a slider that reaches no sound.

Yellow is the flag. Armed, pending, hovered — a thing about to matter. The flag never states; it warns.

type

One heavy sans for everything said, one mono for everything counted. Headings at 900, blocks at 800, labels at 700, body at 500–600. No serif anywhere — this is an instrument, not a program handed out at the door.

chunk

The physical rules. Borders are 3px ink, 4px when the clock or the focus ring grabs something. Shadows are hard offsets — 4px 4px, zero blur — and pressing a control moves it into its own shadow. Plates are square; controls ease to 6px. Nothing interactive is smaller than 48px. Vertical space is cheap; width is not.

border

3px ink everywhere. 4px, in clock red, only where the playhead is.

shadow

Hard offset, no blur. Pressed = translated into the shadow. Nothing floats softly.

radius

0 for plates and grid cells, 6px for controls, a full pill only for chips (nouns).

space · the seven distances

Siblings space with gap, never margins. 48px minimum touch on every control.

This one control replaces every knob. Label above, value below, thick track, a thumb you could grab with a thumb. The cobalt fill is your setting; the green strip beside it is the engine, measured — two facts, two columns, never merged. A moving meter beside a still slider is the page proving the sound is real.

Sliders stack in a labeled column — a voice's strip reads top to bottom: level first, then its three sends (genre, delay, reverb; the main send is the level itself), then any insert wet. Vertical space is cheap. Pages may be tall; they may not be wide.

Keyboard: the invisible native range carries focus; arrow up is more, arrow down is less. The focus ring is a 4px cobalt outline around the track.

A chip is a noun — a place and a year, a genre, a kept melody. Nouns don't blend, so chips are single-choice where they name a basis. The pill is the one rounded shape in the system, reserved for nouns. Chosen = cobalt cap.

tab row

The band's strip: seated voices as solid blocks, empty chairs as dashed + adds at the end. The active tab is a cobalt cap — hand paint, because you chose it. The row wraps; it never scrolls sideways. Switching tabs never moves the page (the anchor law stands).

reaches no sound

An unreachable control goes faint with its reason printed — one line, measured, riding the control as data-why for gates and screen readers alike. Faint is not hidden: the control keeps its place, its label, and its shape. No tooltips, ever — a finger cannot hover.

The clock may write text only inside an element carrying data-live, and a live element never holds a control. Clock red marks the words that move. Everything else on the page is frozen while the record plays — this sentence is the one thing that breathes.

step grid · time flows down

16 rows · tap a cell · the needle reads downward

run it

Top to bottom is always better than left to right. Rows are steps; columns are voices; the row under the needle wears the 4px clock ring and a clock-tint number. A set cell is a cobalt cap — hand paint, because you tapped it. The grid grows downward as the pattern grows; it never grows sideways past its voices.

Only the engraved score may run left to right — notation is the one surface with a horizontal law of its own. Everything else in the app that shows time (rolls, lanes, automation) reads like this grid: down.

voice

"Get rid of all extra text." The rules for when a sentence may appear:

  1. One word for absence: default. as it stands · nothing set · the record's own → default

  2. Say it once. The third column says what stands; the menu, the label and the heading do not repeat it. as it stands — a hall → default, a hall

  3. A refusal is a reason, on the control, in one line. Measured, never vague. 40 is as slow as this box counts

  4. Only the clock speaks in time, and only inside [data-live]. No other text on the page may change while the record plays.

  5. No tooltips. A tooltip is information hidden from a finger. Anything worth saying is on the page; anything not worth the space is deleted.

  6. A sentence must carry state no control shows. Otherwise it goes. looping hook — tb303  ·  riding −3.2 dB seated
