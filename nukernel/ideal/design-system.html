ink

No pure grey anywhere — every neutral leans warm, the soot of a scribble strip. The three lights are semantic, never decorative, and never mixed: a thing is lit because the hand chose it, the clock is on it, or the engine measured it.

the meter

Amber is the hand. Selection, focus, the chosen word in a menu, the active tab. If amber is on it, you put it there.

Lamp is the clock. The playhead, the sounding note on the staff, the [data-live] sentence. If lamp is on it, it is happening right now, and it will move without you.

Signal is the meter. What the engine is actually doing — measured, never trusted. A knob with no green under it is a knob that reaches no sound.

type

Two voices, because the page is two things. The serif is the document — what the record says, in sentences. The mono is the machine — labels, values, bars, hertz. A reader always knows which one is talking.

space

Seven distances, and no others. Controls inside a block sit on the small steps; blocks breathe on the big ones. Siblings space with gap, never margins.

4–8 · The band

The one long scroll survives. Axis headings stay sticky bands — the heading on screen is the axis you are inside — now a raised strip on the panel instead of a pastel wash. Wide things (the board, a tab row, the score) scroll sideways inside their own rail; the page itself never does.

A chip is a noun — a place and a year, a genre, a kept melody. Nouns don't blend, so chips are single-choice where they name a basis. Mono type: a chip is data, not a sentence.

tab row

The band's strip: seated voices in serif, empty chairs as quiet mono + adds at the end of the row. The active tab is amber underlined — hand light, because you chose it. The row scrolls in its own rail; switching tabs never moves the page (the anchor law stands).

An unreachable control greys with its reason printed — one line, faint, measured, riding the control as data-why for gates and screen readers alike. Greyed is not hidden: the control keeps its place and its label. No tooltips, ever — a finger cannot hover.

The clock may write text only inside an element carrying data-live, and a live element never holds a control. Lamp color marks the words that move. Everything else on the page is frozen while the record plays — this sentence is the one thing that breathes.

voice

"Get rid of all extra text." The rules for when a sentence may appear:

  1. One word for absence: default. as it stands · nothing set · the record's own → default

  2. Say it once. The third column says what stands; the menu, the label and the heading do not repeat it. as it stands — a hall → default, a hall

  3. A refusal is a reason, on the control, in one line. Measured, never vague. 40 is as slow as this box counts

  4. Only the clock speaks in time, and only inside [data-live]. No other text on the page may change while the record plays.

  5. No tooltips. A tooltip is information hidden from a finger. Anything worth saying is on the page; anything not worth the space is deleted.

  6. A sentence must carry state no control shows. Otherwise it goes. looping hook — cantor  ·  riding −3.2 dB seated
