The One Board

Five strips, four buses in a line, and the section moves on its face. Every voice carries up to three inserts of its own — what everyone else does with effects — then sends its finished sound to four buses wired in series: genre → delay → reverb → main. No knobs: every continuous value stands up as a slider. Time, where it appears, runs down.

I · the strips

Down a strip reads in signal order: instrument → three inserts (each with its own wet) → four sends, tapped after the inserts → EQ → pan → the fader. Cobalt fill is what you set; the green strip beside a fader is the engine, measured. Five strips sit across a wide room and stack in a narrow one — the page grows tall, never wide.

II · the bus rack · in series

One line, top to bottom: genre fx bus → delay bus → reverb bus → main. Every strip sends its post-insert mix to all four stages; each stage also pours into the next through its own level slider. The genre bus arrives loaded by extraction from the genre's own recipe — and everything it dealt is editable here, in the open.

III · section automation

One fact, two views. The grid is where you set — six words per voice per section, saved with the song. The lanes are where it reads — drawn from the same table, never stored as a curve. Sections run down; the row in clock red is sounding.

vocabulary: out · hush · back · — (as mixed) · fwd · lift. A word is a trim on the strip's fader for that section — deterministic, diffable, thumb-sized. Tap a cell to cycle its word; the lanes follow.

IV · the engine, honestly

  1. The inserts already exist. The per-voice modules are on disk today — engine/faust/dist insert_*, mono, and most already expose a mix param. The three slots on a strip seat what the engine already compiles; the wet slider is that mix param, surfaced.

  2. The series is half-built. fx_bus.dsp already bleeds delay → reverb at a fixed 0.2 constant. This rack generalizes what the engine half-does: the bleed becomes each bus's own level slider, and the genre bus is the one genuinely new stage in the line.

  3. The sends are real and already post-insert. Today they are per-unit u.rev / u.del, tapped after the voice's chain. The four-send row renames and extends that tap — genre and main join delay and reverb.

  4. This reverses a standing decision, on Paul's word. 2026-08-26 said “never embed effects on instruments.” 2026-08-27 says “I think we need to do what everyone else does with effects.” Character now rides per-voice in the inserts; the genre's chain moves to its bus — still dealt by extraction, never typed by hand.

Laws honored: every value is a word or a detent from fields.js; absent spells default and nothing else; cobalt is yours, green is measured, red is now, yellow is armed; a slider with no green beside it is a slider under suspicion; the section grid is score-level data, so gates read it off the rendered record, not this page. New laws kept: time runs down, sliders stand up, no knobs, and no page is wide.
