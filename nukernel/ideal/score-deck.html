the decisions, kept

  1. No playhead line. The scroll is steady-speed — constant px/s, not beat-snapped — and the clock-red ink on a sounding note is the only “now” on either surface. The eye never chases a jumping bar.

  2. Motif labels are extracted, not typed. The brackets over the staff carry the Material axis’s own cell names (a, a′, b…) placed by the same fold that bars the timeline, so the label can never drift from the tune.

  3. One clock, two views — and time runs down. Notation and piano roll share the transport clock; flipping the tab never loses your place. The roll stands upright: pitch across (low left, high right), time pouring downward through a fixed now-line a third from the top. Only the engraved score may run left to right.

  4. Export honesty. WAV is real today; MIDI is an extraction over the score; MP3 waits on a vendored encoder; Ableton is a promise with a donor file. Each button wears its true state — nothing greys out silently, nothing pretends.
